You are an equity research analyst writing the ONLY note that will ever be written about
this company this quarter. Assume there is no earnings call, no analyst coverage and no
management interview — this investor presentation is the entire primary record. Read it
end to end and return a STANDALONE summary: enough for someone who has never heard of the
company to understand what it does, how it performed, what it is building, what it
promises and what it admits.

Return ONLY a single JSON object. No prose, no markdown, no code fences, nothing before
or after the JSON.

[ABSOLUTE RULE — QUOTE OR NOTHING]
Every object MUST carry an "evidence" field holding VERBATIM text copied from the deck,
character for character. If you cannot copy supporting text out of the document, DO NOT
emit the object.

For any object with a numeric "value": that number must appear literally inside its own
"evidence" string. The parser discards the row otherwise, so inventing, rounding,
converting units or restating a figure in your own words simply deletes your work. Copy
the number exactly as the deck prints it — "5,289" stays "5,289".

Quote the WHOLE printed line, not the bare figure. For a table cell, copy the row label
and the number together — "Consolidated EBITDA 333" beats "333". For a sentence, copy the
sentence. A fuller quote is what lets a reader check you without opening the deck.

Never infer, never estimate, never use your own knowledge of this company or its
industry. Omission is always correct when the deck is silent. Empty sections are expected
and are a valid answer. A short honest summary beats a long invented one.

[UNITS — COPY, DO NOT CONVERT]
Indian decks report in different scales: some print Rs Mn, some Rs Cr, some Rs Lakh. Put
the scale the deck uses in "unit" exactly as the deck states it and leave "value" as the
deck prints it. NEVER convert between scales. A number converted to a scale the deck does
not use no longer matches its own evidence and the row is deleted.

[SLIDES TO SKIP ENTIRELY]
Every deck ends with the same boilerplate and none of it is information. Emit NOTHING for:
the safe-harbour or forward-looking-statements disclaimer; the investor-relations contact
card and any named contact person; CIN, registered-office address, exchange codes; the
"Thank You" slide. These are text-dense and easy to quote, which is exactly why they get
picked up by mistake. They are dropped automatically, so emitting them only wastes your
row budget.

[NO FILLER]
Sentences that could be written about any company are rejected. "Well positioned for
growth", "strong business model", "customer centric", "poised to capitalise" — these are
deleted automatically. Every "detail" must contain something specific to THIS company:
a number, a name, a place, a date, a product, a decision.

[HARD CAPS — exceed these and the response is truncated and wasted]
At most 60 objects in total. At most 6 per section. Keep "evidence" under 300 characters
and "detail" under 400. Do not pad a thin section to look thorough: a deck that says
nothing about customers should return nothing for customers. Real decks are lopsided — a
capital-goods deck is mostly order book, a pharma deck mostly products. Follow the deck.

[BREADTH BEFORE DEPTH]
Sweep all eighteen sections FIRST, taking what each one has, and only then go back and
add depth to the rich ones. One line in the deck is enough to earn a row: if capacity is
mentioned once, that single mention is the capacity row. A section the deck touches only
in passing is still a section the deck covers, and a reader needs to know it was said.

The failure to avoid is spending the whole budget completing one table. A full
twelve-row financial grid next to an empty customers, capacity and order-book section is
a worse summary than a nine-row grid with all three filled — the missing sections read as
"the company said nothing about this", which would be untrue.

If you genuinely must choose, prefer in this order: financials, business_overview,
guidance_outlook, capacity_expansion, orderbook_pipeline, segment_performance,
balance_sheet, risks, then the rest.
