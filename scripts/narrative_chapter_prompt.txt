You are writing ONE chapter of a company report on an Indian listed company. The report
reads as one continuous story: your chapter must CONTINUE from the previous chapter and hand
over to the next one. It is not a standalone box.

==================================================================
THE ONE RULE THAT OVERRIDES EVERYTHING
==================================================================
You do not produce numbers. Every figure has already been computed and is listed in the
FACT TABLE below. You may only SELECT facts and EXPLAIN them.
- Any number you write MUST appear in the FACT TABLE. A figure that is not there fails a
  mechanical check and the chapter is rejected.
- You may round for readability (81.7857 -> "82%"). You may NOT add precision.
- Do not compute new numbers: no sums, ratios, growth rates, per-unit figures or conversions.
- ANALYST NOTES (from a forensic deep dive by another model) tell you what matters and give
  qualitative findings. Their numbers are NOT usable unless the same number is in the FACT
  TABLE. Do not copy their tables.

==================================================================
QUALITATIVE CLAIMS NEED EVIDENCE
==================================================================
Every statement that is not a FACT TABLE number and comes from a filing, call or report must
be listed in `claims` with a `source_ref` naming a document id from SOURCE DOCUMENTS and an
`evidence_span` copied VERBATIM from that document (10-40 words, exact substring - it is
checked by string match). If you cannot find a verbatim span, do not make the claim.
Quotations of management must be exact.

==================================================================
HOW TO WRITE IT
==================================================================
1. Open by picking up the thread from the PREVIOUS CHAPTER (one sentence), then make THIS
   CHAPTER'S POINT. End with a sentence that leads into the next chapter's question.
2. Make the dynamics visible: say what drives what. For numbers, say what they mean for the
   business, not just what they are ("margin fell from X to Y as raw material costs rose").
3. Prose only: short paragraphs (max 4 sentences), no headings, no bullet lists, no tables,
   no markdown. Tables are printed separately from the fact pack.
4. NEVER write fact ids, document ids, file names, "Source:", "(see table)", "not disclosed"
   lists or bracket tags in the text. Citations are added automatically from `claims`.
5. If the record is silent on something a reader would expect, put it in `not_disclosed` -
   do not write about the silence in the body unless it IS the finding.
6. If this chapter has nothing real to say, return an empty body. A short honest chapter is
   right; padding is wrong. Never invent.
7. No forecasts, targets or recommendations. Label your own groupings as yours.
8. Plain English; gloss any term a non-specialist would not know the first time it is used.
