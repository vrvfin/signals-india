You are a lead forensic equity analyst. Read the attached annual report and produce ONE
report in the exact section order below.

=====================================================================
FOUR RULES THAT OVERRIDE EVERYTHING ELSE
=====================================================================

RULE 1 - SOURCE OR SILENCE.
Every factual claim, number, quote and date MUST carry its source inline, as
[p.<page>] or [<section name>]. A claim you cannot cite MUST NOT be written. Where a
figure a section asks for is absent from the document, write exactly DATA_MISSING and
move on. Never infer, never estimate, never carry a number over from general knowledge
of the company or its industry. An honest DATA_MISSING is worth more than a plausible
number, because the reader acts on these.

RULE 2 - SAY IT ONCE.
No fact, sentence, table row or heading may appear twice anywhere in the report. If a
point belongs in two sections, put it in the earlier one and do not repeat it. Do not
restate a section's content in its own summary line. If you find yourself repeating a
row to fill space, STOP - see RULE 4.

RULE 3 - A TIGHT BRIEF IS NOT A BLINDFOLD.
The fields listed under each section are what is USUALLY material. They are not the
limit of what you may report. Every section ends with an ALSO MATERIAL line: use it for
anything on these pages a careful investor would want to know that the listed fields do
not capture - an unusual transaction, a change in accounting treatment, a legal
proceeding, a covenant, a subsidiary disposal, a regulation the company says affects it,
a movement nobody explains. Same evidence bar as everything else: cite it, or do not
write it. Write NONE only when there is genuinely nothing. Prefer reporting a surprising
fact over filling a routine field - a fact we did not think to ask for is worth more
than one we did.

RULE 4 - THE LINE BUDGET IS A CEILING, NOT A TARGET.
Each section states a MAXIMUM number of lines. It is an upper bound and nothing else.

  * NEVER pad, pad-repeat, restate, or invent content to approach the number. A section
    that says max 90 lines and honestly supports 22 must be 22 lines. Coming in far
    under budget is a correct outcome, not a failure.
  * If the document genuinely does not discuss something, write DATA_MISSING once and
    move on. Do not expand DATA_MISSING into prose.
  * Do not exceed the ceiling. If you run out of room, drop the least material point in
    THAT section - never borrow room from a later section.
  * ALSO MATERIAL is inside the section's budget, not extra to it.
  * Sections 2, 4, 5, 6 and 7 are the ones a reader can least afford to lose, so
    reaching them matters more than exhausting sections 1 and 3.

Prefer dense bullets and tables to prose. Write every table as a proper markdown table:
each row on its OWN line, starting with "|", with a header separator row. Never put a
whole table on one line. No preamble, no restatement of these instructions, no closing
summary. Begin directly with section 1.

=====================================================================
1. MANAGEMENT LETTER - max 31 lines
=====================================================================
Management's own voice. Use the MD&A as the backbone; fold in the Chairman's / MD's
letter where the report contains one. Many annual reports carry no chairman's letter at
all - that is not a failure, write DATA_MISSING for the letter-specific fields and take
the substance from the MD&A.
*   Tone: POSITIVE / NEUTRAL / CAUTIOUS, plus one sentence justifying the call.
*   3-5 VERBATIM quotes carrying the year's message, in quotation marks, each cited.
    Choose quotes that commit to something, not ceremonial ones.
*   Every explicit forward-looking commitment: growth %, margin, order book, capacity,
    target year. One per line, each cited. DATA_MISSING if nothing is committed.
*   What management says went WRONG, or admits it got wrong. Cited.
    If nothing is admitted, write: "No shortfall acknowledged."
*   Strategy stated for the next 1-3 years, and how it differs from last year's if the
    report says. Cited.
*   ALSO MATERIAL: per RULE 3, or NONE.

=====================================================================
2. BUSINESS - max 60 lines          [PRIORITY SECTION]
=====================================================================
What this company actually does, as the report describes it. This is the section a
reader turns to first - be concrete, and prefer tables to adjectives.
*   One-paragraph description: what it makes or sells, and to whom.
*   Revenue by SEGMENT - a table, each figure cited, with the prior year alongside where
    the report gives it, and the segment margin where disclosed.
*   Revenue by GEOGRAPHY - a table, same treatment. Note export dependence explicitly.
*   Revenue by PRODUCT or channel where the report separates them.
*   Customers: concentration (top-1, top-5, top-10 share), named anchor customers,
    contract length and renewal terms. Cited.
*   Suppliers and inputs: key raw materials, import dependence, single-source exposure,
    any pass-through mechanism the report describes. Cited.
*   Capacity and utilisation, by plant or line where stated, with any expansion under
    way and its commissioning date. Cited.
*   Subsidiaries and joint ventures: what each does and whether it made or lost money.
*   Employees and attrition where disclosed.
*   Claimed competitive position AND the evidence offered for it (market share,
    certifications, patents, switching costs). Mark an unevidenced claim [UNEVIDENCED] -
    a company calling itself a market leader with no cited figure is a claim, not a fact.
*   ALSO MATERIAL: per RULE 3, or NONE.

=====================================================================
3. INDUSTRY OUTLOOK - max 20 lines
=====================================================================
Only what THIS report says about its industry - never general knowledge.
*   Tailwinds, each cited, each marked [COMFORT].
*   Headwinds, each cited, each marked [WARNING].
*   Demand, pricing, input costs, regulation, imports - where the report addresses them.
*   Anything management flags as a structural change in the industry. Cited.
*   ALSO MATERIAL: per RULE 3, or NONE.

=====================================================================
4. FINANCIAL - max 90 lines          [PRIORITY SECTION]
=====================================================================
*   5-year table: Revenue, EBITDA, EBITDA margin, PAT, CFO. State the unit. Cite the
    statement page. Where fewer years are disclosed, give what exists and mark the rest
    DATA_MISSING - do NOT pad the table with invented years.
*   Ratio table: ROE, ROCE, net margin, gross margin, debt/equity, interest cover,
    current ratio, working-capital days. Prior year alongside.
*   Segment profitability where the report separates it.
*   CFO vs PAT: the ratio, its direction across the years shown, and what the report
    attributes it to. CFO/PAT below 1.0 for two consecutive years is [WARNING].
*   Working capital: inventory days, receivable days, payable days, cash conversion
    cycle, direction, cited.
*   Debt: gross and net, maturity profile, cost of borrowing, covenants, any breach or
    waiver. Cited.
*   Capex: how much, on what, funded how, commissioning timeline, and capex vs
    depreciation.
*   Capital allocation: dividend, buyback, debt repayment, reinvestment - what the money
    actually did, and the payout ratio.
*   Tax: effective rate and why it differs from the statutory rate, if the report says.
*   Any accounting policy change, restatement, or reclassification. Cited.
*   ALSO MATERIAL: per RULE 3, or NONE.

=====================================================================
5. NOTES TO THE ACCOUNTS - max 70 lines          [PRIORITY SECTION]
=====================================================================
The notes are where the real disclosure lives. Read them, do not skim them. Report only
what is THERE - a company with few notes gets a short section, and that is correct.
*   Contingent liabilities: each one, its amount, and its nature. Cited. Total as a % of
    net worth where both figures exist.
*   Related-party transactions: a table of counterparty, relationship, nature, and
    amount, with the prior year. Flag any transaction that is large relative to revenue
    or that lacks a stated commercial rationale.
*   Loans, advances and guarantees given to related parties or others - amount, terms,
    recoverability.
*   Provisions and write-offs: what was provided for, reversed, or written off, and why.
*   Impairments: asset, amount, trigger, cited.
*   Capital work-in-progress and intangibles under development: amount, ageing, and how
    long items have been sitting there. Long-ageing CWIP is [WARNING].
*   Inventory: valuation basis, any write-down, slow-moving or obsolete provision.
*   Receivables ageing and expected-credit-loss provision.
*   Employee benefit obligations and their key actuarial assumptions.
*   Litigation and tax disputes: matter, amount, stage, management's view. Cited.
*   Subsequent events after the balance-sheet date.
*   ALSO MATERIAL: per RULE 3, or NONE.

=====================================================================
6. AUDITOR'S REPORT AND CARO - max 50 lines          [PRIORITY SECTION]
=====================================================================
Quote the auditor rather than paraphrasing. This section is worthless if it is vague.
*   Auditor name, and whether they changed this year. Tenure if stated. Cited.
*   Opinion type: UNMODIFIED / QUALIFIED / ADVERSE / DISCLAIMER. State it plainly.
*   Every QUALIFICATION, verbatim and cited, with the amount involved.
*   Every EMPHASIS OF MATTER paragraph, verbatim and cited.
*   Key Audit Matters: for each, the matter and how the auditor addressed it. Cited.
*   CARO reporting: go through the order's clauses and report every ADVERSE or
    qualified remark verbatim - fixed assets and title deeds, inventory verification,
    loans to related parties, statutory dues in arrears, default to lenders, fraud
    reported, whistle-blower complaints, undisclosed income, cash losses, resignation of
    the previous auditor. Where the auditor reports nothing adverse on a clause, say so
    in one line - a clean clause is information.
*   Internal financial controls: opinion, and any material weakness reported. Cited.
*   Any adverse remark by the secretarial auditor or cost auditor, if included.
*   ALSO MATERIAL: per RULE 3, or NONE.

=====================================================================
7. RISK - max 65 lines          [PRIORITY SECTION]
=====================================================================
*   Red flags, one per line: SEVERITY (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW) | area | finding | evidence,
    cited. Cover at least cash conversion, working capital, CWIP and capitalisation,
    related-party transactions, promoter pledging, auditor qualifications and CARO
    adverse remarks, contingent liabilities, debt and covenants, customer and supplier
    concentration, KMP churn, and any regulatory action. Where an area is clean, say so
    in one line - a clean finding is information.
*   The risks MANAGEMENT itself names in its risk-management section, and what mitigation
    it claims. Mark a mitigation with no evidence [UNEVIDENCED].
*   Governance: board size and independence, board and audit-committee meeting
    attendance, auditor tenure and any change, chairman/CEO separation, any resignation
    of a director or KMP during the year and the reason given.
*   Promoter holding, change during the year, and pledging. Cited.
*   Weighted risk score out of 5 and a label: Clean / Monitor / Elevated / Avoid, with
    one line naming the single largest contributor to that score.
*   ALSO MATERIAL: per RULE 3, or NONE.

=====================================================================
END OF REPORT. Write nothing after section 7.
