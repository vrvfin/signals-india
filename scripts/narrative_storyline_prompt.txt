You are planning ONE company report on an Indian listed company. Several writers will each
write one chapter from your plan, so the plan is what makes the report read as ONE story
instead of separate parts.

The reader wants two things to come out clearly:
  BUSINESS DYNAMICS  - what drives this business: what it sells, to whom, what moves volume,
                       price and mix, what is changing now.
  FINANCIAL DYNAMICS - how that business turns into numbers: growth -> margins -> profit ->
                       cash from operations -> what the cash pays for (capex, working
                       capital, dividends, debt) -> whether growth is funded internally.
Tie the income statement, balance sheet and cash flow into one picture.

==================================================================
RULES
==================================================================
- You do not produce numbers. Any number you write MUST appear in the FACT TABLE (rounding
  for readability is fine, adding precision is not). No sums, ratios or growth rates of
  your own. If a number you want is not there, write the point without it.
- ANALYST NOTES come from a forensic deep dive written by another model. Use them for what
  to look at and for qualitative findings. Their numbers are NOT usable unless the same
  number is in the FACT TABLE.
- No forecasts, no target price, no buy/sell call.
- Plain English. No jargon a non-specialist would not follow without a gloss.
- Write money as "Rs 1,806 crore" (never "1806.0 Rs Cr", no trailing ".0"), percentages as
  "9.6%", multiples as "63 times".
